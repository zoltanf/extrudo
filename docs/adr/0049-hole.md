# ADR-0049: Hole

- **Status:** Accepted, 2026-09-30
- **Task:** P3-04 (FR-FT-07). Code: `packages/core/src/hole.ts` (the feature,
  its numbers and defaults, the presets), the evaluator
  `packages/kernel/src/features/hole.ts`, the dialog
  `apps/web/src/features/hole.ts`; the framework hooks in
  `apps/web/src/features/{spec,dialog,planePicker}.ts`; sketch points in the
  model view (`selection/filter.ts`, `selection/pick.ts`,
  `viewport/Sketches.tsx`, `SketchOutputData.points`); the click point from
  `sketch/facePick.ts`. **No facade change** (the OCCT input hash is
  unchanged), no migration; one new feature type,
  documented in `docs/file-format.md` (6.17).
- **Builds on:** ADR-0032 (primitives: planar faces swept by `namedRevolve`,
  placement on a plane or flat face, the plane picker), ADR-0028/-0029
  (`operate` and `splitSolids`), ADR-0005 (names), ADR-0027 (dialogs),
  ADR-0026 (model-mode picking), ADR-0040 (construction planes), ADR-0047
  (patterns replay a feature's tool).

## Context

A hole is the most common thing a 3D-printing designer adds after the
basic body: a screw clearance, a counterbore for the head, a countersink for
a flat head, a blind pocket for a heat-set insert. FR-FT-07 asks for holes
placed on a face at sketch points or at a clicked point, three shapes, blind
or through, a drill-point angle and presets. B5 (the PCB enclosure with screw
posts and countersunk lid screws) needs holes at several points, patterned
and mirrored.

## Decision

### 1. One feature type, `hole`, that always cuts

`hole` is its own feature type (and tool), not a cylinder with an operation:
its inputs are a hole's (diameter, depth, drill point, counterbore, countersink),
its faces have hole names, its messages speak of holes, and the dialog shows
only the fields that apply. It **always cuts**: there is no `operation`
input, so a hole that joins can't be stored. Patterns and mirrors repeat it
as a feature (ADR-0047): its cut tool is what `operate` returns as
`previewTools[0]` with style `cut`; `PATTERNABLE_FEATURE_TYPES` lists it and
`repeatableFeatures` treats a `hole` as a cut although it has no `operation`
input.

Inputs (`HoleInputs`; all optional, `{}` is a 5 mm blind hole 10 mm deep on XY):
`plane`, `points`, `x`, `y`, `type` (`simple`, `counterbore`, `countersink`),
`extent` (`blind`, `through`), `diameter`, `depth`, `tipAngle`, `cbDiameter`,
`cbDepth`, `csDiameter`, `csAngle`, `flip`. Numbers are `expr` inputs as always
(`<ExpressionInput>`, model parameters `dN`).

### 2. The solid is a turned half section, built in TypeScript

Each hole is one polygon in the half plane beside its axis (u across, v along
the drilling direction), turned a whole turn by `namedRevolve`, as the sphere
is (ADR-0032): no facade method, no OCCT input change, no CI wait for the
WASM. `holeSection` is pure and tested on its own. The sections:

| | outline from the axis at the plane |
|---|---|
| simple, blind | out to the radius, down, in to a point `r / tan(tip / 2)` beyond the depth (or a flat floor for 0°) |
| counterbore | out to the step, down, in to the hole, then as simple |
| countersink | out to the cone's diameter, down to the hole's radius over `(D - d) / 2 / tan(angle / 2)`, then as simple |
| through | the wall runs past every body: to the far corner of each body's box plus 1 mm |

The holes of one feature go into one tool (`mergeTools` from the pattern
code: holes that touch are fused, the rest become one compound) and one
`operate(cut)` does the boolean, so twenty holes cost one boolean, and the
result is split into bodies with `splitSolids` if a hole cuts a bar in two.

- **Depth** is measured from the plane to the end of the *full diameter*; the
  drill point's cone comes on top. A 10 mm hole in a 10 mm plate therefore
  leaves the point outside the plate and is a through hole, which is what
  "depth" means in a drawing. A counterbore's depth is from the plane too, and
  must be less than a blind hole's; a countersink is given by its diameter at
  the surface and its full angle (90° metric, 82° imperial), and its depth
  follows.
- **Direction.** Into a face: against its outward normal. From an origin or
  construction plane: against its normal (down from XY). `flip` reverses it.
  The plane has no material side, so there is no automatic choice; a hole that
  only touches the body ("doesn't remove anything") names Flip in its message.

### 3. Placement: a plane, then a click or sketch points

- **A clicked point** is stored as `x`, `y` in the plane's sketch frame
  (`faceSketchFrame`, as a primitive's placement), never as world
  coordinates, so it follows the face when the model changes. The dialog
  learns the click through two small framework additions: the plane picker
  hands the world point where the pick ray met the face or plane
  (`SketchTarget.at`, `PlanePicker.onPick(_, at)`), and a spec may have
  `placeAt(world, values)` turn it into values (here X and Y, and it lets go of
  sketch points). With `placeAt` a click **adds** the plane or face instead of
  toggling it, so clicking the same face again moves the point. A face
  pre-selected before the tool proposes its centre. Rounded to 0.01 mm: a
  clicked point is arbitrary, and `12.34 mm` reads better than `12.3398…`.
- **Sketch points** are `sketchEntity` references to points
  (`<sketch>/<point>`), which the ref kind always allowed. The sketch's output
  now carries `points` (`SketchOutputData.points`, sketch coordinates), so the
  kernel needs no sketch data. A point **drops along the plane's normal onto
  the plane**: the sketch may be on the face, on another parallel plane, or
  across the model, and the plane says where the hole starts. With no plane
  picked, the dialog proposes the plane of the first point's sketch.
  Picking them in the view needed three pieces: a selection-filter key
  `sketchPoints` that is off by default, is not in the nav bar's list and is
  turned on only by a dialog field with `sketchPoints: true` (which then
  takes points and no curves); `pick.ts` accepts points under that key; and
  a sketch that isn't being edited draws its points (and the picked ones as
  dots in the accent) while such a field picks (`SketchDrawing.showPoints`).
- The dialog shows X and Y only while no points are picked. Two points in one
  place make one hole, with a warning. At most 200 holes.

### 4. Names

A hole's faces are `hole:<feature>:side:<part>`: `wall`, `tip`, `floor`,
`cbwall`, `cbfloor`, `cone` (the top and the bottom of a through hole vanish
in the cut). With sketch points every part is prefixed with the point's
entity ID (`<point>.wall`): adding or removing another point leaves the
others' names alone, and a fillet or chamfer on a hole's rim, named from
`hole:H:side:wall`, survives a change of diameter or depth (tested). A click
hole has plain parts. A pattern's copies are `pattern:<id>:<label>:from:(hole:…)`
as for any feature (ADR-0047).

### 5. Messages

Errors (OK stays disabled, the chip shows ✕) say what to change: "The
diameter must be greater than 0." "The counterbore diameter (5 mm) must be
larger than the hole's (6 mm)." "The counterbore (12 mm) is as deep as the hole
(10 mm). Make the hole deeper or the counterbore shallower." "The drill
point angle must be from 0° (flat) to less than 180°." A face that isn't
flat says so. A deleted sketch point is a lost reference (Fix References
offers it). **A hole that cuts nothing** is the cut's own error ("The cut
doesn't touch any body" / "doesn't remove anything", plus "Check where it
sits, how deep it goes and its direction (Flip).") when no hole of the
feature cuts, and a **warning** ("2 of 5 holes don't reach a body, so they cut
nothing. Check the points.") when only some miss: five points with one off the
part still make four holes.

### 6. Presets fill the fields; nothing about them is stored

`HOLE_PRESETS` (core) lists what choosing a preset fills: number
expressions and, for inserts, the type and extent. The dialog's Preset
dropdown is **not an input**: the stored inputs are the plain values, so a
revision of the table never changes an old design. The dropdown mirrors the
values (`presetOf`): it shows the preset whose numbers (those that apply to
the current type and extent) all match, else Custom, both when a preset is
chosen and after any edit, and when an existing hole is edited. The framework
gained `FeatureDialogSpec.onChange(field, values)`, pure, whose answer counts
as the user's (so `propose` leaves it alone).

- **Clearance: the normal fit** of ISO 273's medium series, the size a bolt
  passes through with room: M2 2.4, M2.5 2.9, M3 3.4, M4 4.5, M5 5.5, M6 6.6,
  M8 9 mm. Close fit (M3 3.2, M4 4.3, ...) and coarse are left to a user's own
  number: a printed hole comes out a little small, and normal is the one that
  lets a screw go through without reaming. With each, the counterbore of an
  ISO 4762 socket head (head diameter and height plus 0.5 and 0.3 mm: M3
  Ø6 × 3.3, M4 Ø7.5 × 4.3, ...) and the countersink of an ISO 10642 flat head
  (M3 Ø6.7, M4 Ø9, ... at 90°).
- **Heat-set inserts**: a flat-bottomed blind hole a little under the insert's
  outside diameter and deep enough for the insert plus the melted plastic:
  M2 Ø3.2 × 4.5, M2.5 Ø3.6 × 5.5, M3 Ø4.0 × 6.5, M4 Ø5.6 × 8.5, M5 Ø6.4 ×
  10.5 mm. These are **typical values for common brass inserts**, not a
  standard; check them against the inserts you buy (an open item is a
  vendor-specific list).

### 7. The dialog

Plane (picked like Create Sketch's, XY by default; a click also places), Points
(sketch points; "Or pick sketch points"), X and Y, Preset, Type, Extent,
Diameter, Depth and Drill point (blind), the counterbore's or countersink's
two numbers, Flip. The live preview draws the cut over the model; arrows drag
the diameter, the depth, the counterbore's diameter and depth and the
countersink's diameter, on the first hole. The tool's key is H.
`FeatureDialog` counts a points field as "2 points" (`SelectionField.noun`).

## Alternatives rejected

- **A facade `hole` method** (`BRepFeat_MakeCylindricalHole`, or a cylinder
  plus cones built in C++). It would cost a WASM build per change, and
  `BRepFeat_MakeCylindricalHole` has no counterbore or countersink and gives
  no face history. The turned section is ten lines of geometry and gets its
  names from `namedRevolve`.
- **A cylinder primitive with a `cut` operation, per hole** (what the tests of
  ADR-0047 use as "a hole"). No counterbore, no drill point, no presets, names
  and messages that speak of cylinders, and one boolean per hole.
- **Holes as a mode of a general "Cut" tool**, or a hole that can join. There is
  nothing to join, and a stored `operation` a hole ignores is a trap.
- **World coordinates for a click.** They don't follow a face that moves
  when the design changes; a frame-relative X and Y do, and they are plain
  editable numbers (and parameters).
- **Picking a whole sketch as the placement** ("every point of Sketch1").
  It hides which points, and a sketch with circles and lines has more points
  than holes. Sketch points are picked one by one; a circle's centre is one.
- **Making sketch points always pickable in model mode.** They are picked by
  accident between the curves they end, and a face's click would compete with
  them. A field-only filter key keeps everyday selection as it was.
- **Storing the preset's ID** as an input. It would make the dropdown
  authoritative over the numbers, need a migration story for changed tables,
  and show a preset after the numbers were edited.
- **Depth to the tip** of the drill point. A drawing gives the depth of the
  full diameter and a 118° point adds 0.3 × the diameter; users reading
  "depth 10" on a 10 mm plate expect a through hole, not a pocket with a tip
  poking out.

## Open items

- **Bodies to cut** (participants): a hole cuts every body it touches. A
  `bodies` input like the primitives' would restrict it.
- **Several clicked points in one feature.** A click hole is one; more holes
  come from sketch points or a pattern of the hole.
- **Sketch points of a hidden sketch** can't be picked, and an extrude hides
  the sketch it used: show the sketch first (the dialog's hint says so).
  Picking through "Select other…" doesn't offer points either.
- **Counterbore and countersink together**, tapped or threaded holes (cosmetic
  threads), slots, holes on curved faces (the face must be flat), holes
  normal to a face from a point off it.
- **The marking menu has no Hole wedge** (the slots are full: ADR-0042);
  Hole is in Create's menu and on H.
- **Presets**: close and coarse fits, inches (#4-40...), vendor lists of
  inserts, a user's own presets, and FR-3DP-05's document-level `tolerance`
  parameter that hole presets would add to their diameters (Phase 4).
- **Direction on a plane** could follow the side of the body nearest to it.
- The dialog is tall for a counterbored blind hole with a preset (it
  scrolls); grouping the sizes is a polish item.
